# Make improvement compound without allowing process to take over

Turn useful lessons into the narrowest durable owner. A principle has a high
admission bar: broad applicability, durable meaning, and consistently desirable
consequences. It should contribute independent judgment, not merely restate a
consequence derived from existing principles. Preserve useful derivations in
applications or skills so they need not be repeated. When uncertain, start in a
focused skill and learn from applying it. Promotion follows demonstrated
breadth, not the importance of one incident.

Scope determines where knowledge belongs. Use a deterministic tool or lint when
it can faithfully enforce a rule at worthwhile cost; use a skill or rubric when
judgment remains. A cheap skill or script can test an immature rule before
larger investment. Principles guide judgment; encode their concrete consequences
without claiming to automate the judgment itself. There is no required sequence
of artifacts or eventual obligation to replace a rubric with a tool.

A focused skill owns its method; the project composing skills owns how they
combine.

Apply the same scrutiny to the engineering system and its improvement harness as
to product code. Process must demonstrate value greater than its adoption and
maintenance burden. Do not preserve a metric that improves while the real
outcome does not, or schedule review that cannot change a decision.

Keep autonomous improvement bounded by authority, budget, gates, stop rules, and
rollback. A harness needs an external witness for its own improvement; it must
not approve itself, silently weaken gates, or redefine its governing goals. Use
measured gains to make future work easier, not to justify endless work on the
process.

Offload recurring cognitive work into verified tools when the saved reasoning,
errors, and future capacity justify construction, verification, maintenance,
execution, and comprehension costs. Reuse the investment rather than asking
humans or agents to reconstruct the same global argument on every change.
Stronger agents do not remove this reason to use tools. Dan expects faster
agents to make early, local mechanical feedback increasingly valuable; that
expectation is a motivation, not a measured law about model capability or
verification speed. Keep the assurance scope explicit and optimize the actual
feedback bottleneck.

Make engineering judgments teachable by identifying the distinction behind a
preference, its expected outcome, and what evidence could change it. Encode
understood distinctions in the narrowest useful constraint, test, tool, or skill
so attention can move to the judgments still requiring expertise. Partial
capture is valuable; do not invent precision for the remaining art. Prefer
comparisons that yield testable competing hypotheses over arguments that stop at
different tastes.

Let models expose candidate rules; Dan decides whether those rules express his
judgment before they become policy. Usually observe a judgment recur a few times
before generalizing it. A single decision supplies evidence, not automatically a
universal rule.

Distinguish authority to make a decision from authority to change the policy
that judges it. Dan would fully delegate some classes of questions to an LLM and
correct disagreements as they appear; other decisions remain human. Do not turn
policy acceptance into mandatory human approval of every delegated choice. The
classes and their boundaries remain unresolved, so do not infer unrestricted
authority from this preference.

Probe what the existing acceptance criteria still permit. An unsatisfactory
result that passes the gates can expose a missing distinction in the model,
rubric, or checks. Test an accepted refinement before preserving it as a new
baseline.

Apply this scrutiny recursively. The SDLC is itself a model with assumptions,
transitions, and feedback; these principles must earn their own context and
maintenance costs. Keep the common decision path simple, while giving an
exception prominence proportional to both frequency and consequence. A rare
catastrophic exception must remain hard to miss.
