# Distinguish provisional work from publishable assurance

Match verification to the work's declared maturity. A local checkpoint, a
direction review, and a release-ready artifact are different claims.

Full publication assurance on every speculative edit can impede learning, while
a provisional checkpoint must not masquerade as verified work.

For Dan, TDD is a tool rather than the governing process. Known types and
invariants can precede behavioral tests; agents may develop tests and
implementation together.
A failing reproducer is especially useful for a bug. Choose the feedback that
teaches the needed fact, then shape final history and required evidence for
human review regardless of exploratory order.

Retained checkpoints meet their declared maturity gates; published commits meet
the publication bar. Reuse evidence only for the actual checked content and
scope.

**An MVP reduces features, not required assurance.** Define success and its
minimum dependencies, release the usable core, then iterate. Tests, lints, and
other required checks are part of delivery. A proof of concept can deliberately
relax verification to learn, but that is a different maturity claim from a
minimum viable product intended for real use.
